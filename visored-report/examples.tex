        Prove that for any integer $k > 2$, there are no positive integers $x$, $b$, and $y$ that satisfy:
        $$x^k + (b+1)^k = y^k$$
    \end{tcolorbox}

    Simplified proof:
    \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=blue!10, width=\linewidth]
        This follows directly from Fermat's Last Theorem.
    \end{tcolorbox}

Regularized proof:
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=red!10, width=\linewidth]
    Let $k$ be an integer.
    Assume $k>2$.
    Let $x$ be a positive integer.
    Let $b$ be a positive integer.
    Let $y$ be a positive integer.
    The goal is to prove that there are no positive integers $x$, $b$, and $y$ that satisfy $x^k + {{(b+1)}}^k = y^k$.
    We have for any integer $k > 2$, there are no positive integers $x$, $b$, and $y$ that satisfy $x^k + {{(b+1)}}^k = y^k$ by Fermat's Last Theorem.
\end{tcolorbox}
